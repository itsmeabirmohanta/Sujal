Find pin 1 before you solder

  1. Pin 1 carries GPIO54. Check the board's printed pin labels.
  2. Confirm with a multimeter on beep: the USB‑C shell is ground and beeps to pins 3, 8, 13, 18, 23, 28, 33, 38.
  3. Pin 40 (VBUS) sits directly across from pin 1, followed by 39 (VSYS) and 38 (GND).
  4. On the perfboard, set the two header rows 7 holes apart (17.78 mm), the same as a Raspberry Pi Pico.

Rules that protect the board

The first four can kill the P4‑Pico or a sensor. The rest save you a confusing afternoon.

Must

VSYS (pin 39) takes 5 V only

Feed it +5V_LOGIC from J8. The P4‑Pico's 3.3 V regulator needs at least 4.5 V, and the other parts on that rail are 5 V parts. Never the 8 V rail, never the battery.

Must

Pin 36 (3V3 out) stays unconnected

The sensors keep running from the POWER board's 3V3_B, as before. Joining the P4‑Pico's own 3.3 V to 3V3_B puts two regulators against each other.

Must

R6 pulls the hall line up to 3.3 V, not 5 V

The P4 is not 5 V tolerant. If your hall sensor is a module with its own pull‑up resistor to VCC, remove that resistor or power the module from 3V3_B instead.

Must

Don't ground pin 37 (3V3_EN)

It switches off the P4‑Pico's 3.3 V supply. Leave it floating: the board already pulls it up.

Bench

USB‑C and the stack

With the stack switched off, USB‑C power flows out through VSYS into the 5 V logic rail and every board on it. Flash with J8 unplugged, or use a data‑only (VBUS‑cut) cable while the stack is powered. Your laptop is safe either way: the P4‑Pico blocks current flowing back into USB.

Parts

Capacitor ratings

The 470 µF on the 8 V rail needs a 16 V or higher rating; the 5 V rails take 10 V or higher. The striped side goes to GND.

Skip

Pins 19, 20, 21, 22 and 24

19 and 20 are the chip's built‑in USB‑JTAG pair. 21, 22 and 24 (GPIO48, 47, 46) share the microSD card's switchable supply, so their voltage depends on firmware.

Flight

Lock it down for 30 g

Female headers are fine on the bench. For flight, strap the P4‑Pico to the perfboard with a cable tie or hot glue at both header ends, and put a dab of hot glue where every wire leaves its pad.

Ground and wire on perfboard

The PCB had a full ground pour. On perfboard you build that ground yourself, and where the heavy current returns matters.

  • Wire sizes

    +8V_STEER and its ground20 AWG
    +5V_SERVO and its ground22 AWG
    +5V_LOGIC, +3V3_B24–26 AWG
    All signal wires28–30 AWG

  • Suggested colours

    GND + rails servo, ESC UART I2C, hall

    Write the rail name beside each red bus with a marker: four positive rails look identical once soldered.

  • Servo headers

    Mark − + S beside every 3‑pin header. MG90S leads run brown, red, orange in that order.

Power budget and firmware pins

Two things change outside the soldering: how much the 5 V logic rail must supply, and the pin numbers in your flight code.

Plan about 0.3 A on +5V_LOGIC

Espressif asks for a 3.3 V supply that can deliver at least 380 mA to the ESP32‑P4, flash and PSRAM included. Through the P4‑Pico's buck regulator, at roughly 90 % efficiency:

3.3 V × 0.38 A ÷ 0.90 ÷ 5 V ≈ 0.28 A peak at 5 V

Check that the POWER board's 5 V logic regulator has that headroom on top of everything else it feeds. Then measure the real current into VSYS during bring‑up and update the 2‑hour pad budget with it.

Pin map for the flight code

// VRITRA Board 4 - ESP32-P4-Pico pin map (replaces Teensy 4.0)
// hdr = P4-Pico header pin, T = old Teensy pin

// Things that move: servo PWM; ESC on PWM, or RMT for DShot
#define PIN_STEER_L      18   // hdr 4  -> J10 S   (T 2)
#define PIN_STEER_R      17   // hdr 5  -> J11 S   (T 3)
#define PIN_BAY_LID      16   // hdr 6  -> J12 S   (T 4)
#define PIN_PETRI        15   // hdr 7  -> J13 S   (T 5)
#define PIN_SERVO_X      14   // hdr 9  -> J16 S   (T 10)
#define PIN_ESC           6   // hdr 10 -> J14 S   (T 6)

// Sensors on the P4-Pico's own I2C bus (2.2k pull-ups on board)
#define PIN_I2C_SDA       7   // hdr 17            (T 18)
#define PIN_I2C_SCL       8   // hdr 16            (T 19)
#define I2C_ADDR_BMP585   0x47  // SDO tied to 3V3_B
#define I2C_ADDR_LSM6DSO  0x6A  // DO tied to GND
// 0x18 also answers: the ES8311 audio codec on the P4-Pico
#define PIN_HALL         32   // hdr 26, 10k pull-up to 3V3_B (T 9)

// Links. UART0 (GPIO37/38) is the USB-C console: keep it free.
#define PIN_GNSS_RX      23   // hdr 31  UART1 RX           (T 0)
#define PIN_LINK_TX      20   // hdr 35  UART2 TX -> MAIN + BACKUP (T 8)
#define PIN_MAIN_RX      21   // hdr 34  UART2 RX <- MAIN   (T 7)
#define PIN_BACKUP_RX    22   // hdr 32  UART3 RX <- BACKUP (T 15)
  • GNSS and BACKUP ports only receive. Give them an RX pin and leave TX unassigned. BACKUP hears PIN_LINK_TX, the same shared line Teensy TX2 drove.
  • Turn on the internal pull‑ups on the three RX pins, so a loose cable idles high instead of feeding noise into a parser.
  • Command every servo to its safe position as the first thing in setup.
