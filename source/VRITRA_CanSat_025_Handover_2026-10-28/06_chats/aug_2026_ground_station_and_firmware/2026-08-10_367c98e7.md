## The beacon — this needed a complete redesign

`digitalWrite()` and `tone()` **both produce silence** on a MAX98357A. It has no analogue input and no oscillator — it decodes I2S and mutes itself if the clocks stop. The old `#ifdef PIN_BEACON digitalWrite(...)` could never have made a sound.

New `vritra_fc/Beacon.h` + [Beacon.cpp](vritra_fc/Beacon.cpp), following your `Motor.h`/`Wheel` module pattern.

**Pins you asked me to assign — use SAI2, not the default:**

| MAX98357A | Teensy | Why |
|---|---|---|
| DIN | **2** | SAI2 OUT2 |
| LRC | **3** | SAI2 LRCLK2 |
| BCLK | **4** | SAI2 BCLK2 |
| SD | **37** | GPIO enable/shutdown |

The Audio library's *default* output (SAI1) is fixed at pins 7/20/21/23 — pin 7 is **Serial2 RX, your GNSS**, and pin 20 is **PIN_BUS_V**, the only battery measurement on the vehicle. Using it would cost you both. SAI2's pins are free.

Two things that would have cost you bench hours: **SD_MODE is a voltage-coded channel select**, not a plain enable (3.3 V HIGH = left channel only), so I feed the tone to both channels — a mono amp fed the unselected channel is silent and looks exactly like a dead part. And SD is driven **low in `begin()` before anything else**, or the beacon shrieks through every power-up. `setup()` fires a 250 ms chirp so each boot audibly proves the whole chain.

**The part cannot meet the requirement, and I'm not going to tune around it.** Your requirement is **≥ 92 dB(A) at 1 m**. The SM400204-1's 99 dB is quoted at **100 mm** — free-field spreading is 20 dB per decade, so that's **79 dB at 1 m**, or ~84 dB(A) reading the rating generously and driving the amp's full 3.2 W. Closing 8–13 dB needs roughly 20× the acoustic power, which is in neither the 4 W speaker nor the 3.2 W amp. **That needs a louder transducer — a piezo siren or horn — not a firmware change.**

What firmware *can* do, it does: the tone is a 2.8/3.5 kHz warble rather than sitting on the speaker's 220 Hz resonance. Resonance is where the driver is most *efficient*, but A-weighting docks 220 Hz by ~11 dB and gives 3 kHz +1 dB — about 12 dB better on the meter that grades you. Pulsed 1.4 s on / 0.6 s off, because a changing tone is far easier to walk toward and it halves the ~0.7 A draw from the 5 V rail.

Fit **≥100 µF bulk capacitance at the amp's supply pins** — same lesson the LR1121 PA taught you, and a Class-D amp is worse because its draw is continuous rather than bursty.
