**CLAUDE** (2026-08-07T10:16:12)

0xFF on all three bytes means the flash isn't answering at all. Three things to check in order:

**1. Swap MOSI and MISO — most likely cause.**
Your module probably labels them `DO` and `DI`, not MOSI/MISO. The naming is from the chip's perspective:
```
Module DO  (data OUT of chip)  →  Teensy pin 12  (MISO)
Module DI  (data IN  to chip)  →  Teensy pin 11  (MOSI)
```
If you wired it the other way round, the chip receives nothing and MISO floats high → you get 0xFF. Just swap the two wires and retry.

**2. Check 3.3V is actually reaching the module.** Measure with a multimeter between VCC and GND on the module. If it reads 0V the chip can't answer.

**3. Confirm CS is toggling.** Add this temporarily right before the `jedec()` call in setup to visually confirm pin 5 is working:

```cpp
// quick blink test — remove after confirming
for (int i = 0; i < 5; i++) {
    csLow();  delay(100);
    csHigh(); delay(100);
}
```

If the on-board LED on pin 13 flickers during this (because SCK shares it), the SPI bus itself is alive. If not, SPI.begin() may not have initialised — but that's rare.

Swap DO/DI first. That's the cause in 9 out of 10 cases.
