Yes — specifically **HFSS**, and one simulation is worth more than all the others combined.

## The one that matters: aluminium plates vs your antenna

This is exactly the unknown I flagged. λ/4 at 867 MHz is 86.5 mm and your vehicle is 150 mm wide, so the aluminium *will* interact with the element — the question is how much, and where the nulls land. HFSS answers that precisely, and it's a sub-wavelength problem (0.43λ × 0.65λ), so it's small and fast.

What you'd get out:
- **Realized gain at 867 MHz for *your* vehicle** — replaces the vendor's unverified 3.58 dBi with a real number that feeds straight back into `ANT_GAIN_CANSAT_DBI` and the link budget
- **3D pattern with the null map** — tells you where to mount the element, and what a tumbling descent actually costs you
- **S11/VSWR at 867** — how far the aluminium detunes it

You can test five mounting positions in a day. That's the thing a bench test can't do cheaply.

## Priority after that

**2. Antenna-to-antenna isolation, 867 MHz TX → GNSS L1 at 1575 MHz.** Classic CanSat killer: a +14 dBm transmitter tens of mm from a receiver listening at −130 dBm. If GPS drops lock every time you transmit, this is why. It also produces the artifact §6.vi mandates — *"teams will be required to present the radio interference mitigation plan and strategy during the PDR stage"* — and you don't have one yet.

**3. Icepak — sealed-enclosure thermal.** You logged 49.6 °C CPU in open air. §5.2.i requires the electronics enclosed, and a Teensy 4.1 at 600 MHz plus the LR1121 PA plus two buck converters in a sealed aluminium tube with no convection will run hotter. Worth knowing before it throttles or the BMP585 reads thermal drift as altitude.

**4. SIwave/Q3D — only once you have a real PCB.** This would finally settle the 400 kHz I²C question with numbers instead of the current "it broke, so we stay at 100 kHz." Simulate 2.2 kΩ pull-ups against real trace capacitance and find out whether 400 kHz is actually available to you.

## Three things that will actually block you

**Your Fusion model has 941 bodies. You cannot feed that to HFSS.** You need a separate, deliberately crude EM model: aluminium plates, shell, GFRP rods, antenna element, and plain blocks where the PCBs and battery sit. **You don't have the RadioMaster's internal geometry.** Model an equivalent (sleeve or half-wave dipole tuned to 867), validate it in free space against the vendor's 3.58 dBi first, *then* add the structure. You're measuring the delta the aluminium causes, which is the number you actually need.

**Student licence caps you at 64,000 3D mesh elements** ([HFSS Student Limitations](https://ansyshelp.ansys.com/public//Views/Secured/Electronics/v252/en/Subsystems/HFSS/Content/GettingStarted/HFSSStudentLimitations.htm), [free download](https://www.ansys.com/academic/students/ansys-electronics-desktop-student)). ## One thing worth knowing

Your file already attributed `-707` to a **different** cause — fact #2, `setRfSwitchTable()` being cleared by `begin()`. That was also real, debugged months earlier.

So `-707` has had **two unrelated causes** on this hardware. `RADIOLIB_ERR_SPI_CMD_FAILED` only means "the status byte wasn't a success code" — it says nothing about why. I documented both rather than overwriting one with the other, because next time someone hits `-707` they need to know it's ambiguous, not chase the wrong one for a day.

## What I added

**`rfErrName()`** — boot now prints `code=-707 — SPI_CMD_FAILED (status byte was not OK)` instead of a bare integer.

**`diagnoseSpi()`** in [Radio.cpp](vritra_fc/Radio.cpp) — runs only when `begin()` has already failed. It resets the chip, issues a raw `GetVersion` (opcode `0x0101`), and classifies what comes back on MISO:

| Raw bytes | Verdict |
|---|---|
| all `0xFF` | **The exact bug you hit.** MCU listening to a floating pin — `setMISO()` lost, or the joint is open |
| all `0x00` | MISO shorted low, or module unpowered |
| BUSY stuck high | Chip not booting — check RESET polarity and 3V3 |
| mixed | **Chip is talking.** Wiring is fine — look at the RF switch table, `TCXO_V`, or the frequency/power args |

It also dumps the live pin numbers, so a wrong `Config.h` is visible in the same breath.

That last row is the valuable one: it tells you when to *stop* suspecting wiring. That's the distinction that cost you the day.

**Gateway** — added the same error naming to [vritra_gs.ino](vritra_gs/vritra_gs.ino), with a note that this specific failure **cannot occur there**. ESP32's `SPI.begin(SCK, MISO, MOSI, CS)` takes pins as explicit arguments, so there's no default pin to be left behind. If the gateway reports `-707`, it's the switch table or a genuinely dead module.

## The load-bearing detail I made explicit

`setMISO`/`setMOSI`/`setSCK` **must all precede `SPI1.begin()`** — afterwards the peripheral is already latched to the old pins and the calls are silently ineffective. That ordering is now commented in the code as load-bearing, because it's the kind of thing that looks like harmless reordering during a cleanup.

## One honest caveat

I allowed it on the grounds that it only ever runs on a boot path that has already failed and is about to `return false` — there's no cadence left to protect. It's stated as such in the comment so it doesn't read as an accidental violation. Say the word if you'd rather it be a state machine.
