    <fluid_analysis>
        Prefer ANSYS Fluent if it is installed, licensed, and suitable for
        the required aerodynamic model. Evaluate other available solvers
        only when technically justified.

        Identify the appropriate steady-state or transient formulation,
        turbulence treatment, fluid domain, inlet and outlet boundaries,
        ground or symmetry conditions where applicable, and the necessary
        near-canopy mesh resolution.

        Model the ram-air parachute as the geometry and physics permit.
        A rigid-canopy external-flow model may provide useful aerodynamic
        information, but it cannot by itself establish flexible-canopy
        inflation, fabric deformation, or deployment reliability.
    </fluid_analysis>

    <structural_analysis>
        Prefer ANSYS Mechanical when it is installed, licensed, and
        appropriate for the available geometry and material information.

        Assess canopy panels, suspension lines, seams, risers, attachment
        points, deployment hardware, and the payload interface. Select
        appropriate structural idealizations and contact or connection
        representations.

        Evaluate stress, strain, deformation, load transfer, and failure
        indicators only to the extent supported by the chosen material
        models and structural representation.
    </structural_analysis>

    <coupled_analysis>
        Determine whether fluid-structure interaction is necessary and
        technically feasible. If it is required but unavailable, document
        the limitation and develop a defensible alternative analysis plan.

        Distinguish static structural loading, transient structural
        response, external-flow aerodynamics, inflation dynamics, and
        coupled fluid-structure behavior. Do not claim that one analysis
        proves all of these phenomena.
    </coupled_analysis>

    <deployment_analysis>
        Treat deployment, inflation, and opening shock as separate
        high-priority engineering questions. Identify the data and solver
        capabilities required to assess them.

        the additional modeling or physical testing required.
