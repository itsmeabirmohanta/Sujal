That's fine. Nothing is damaged, the INA260 just isn't in the circuit yet. You have two choices.

## Option 1 (recommended): put the INA260 into the + wire
This measures **voltage and current**, so the GUI can show battery %, power use and discharge.

1. **Unsolder (or cut) the wire** between the **switch output** and the **buck input +**.
2. Solder **switch output → INA260 IN+** (pin 7).
3. Solder **INA260 IN− (pin 8) → buck input +**. If the other bucks or the ESC take + from that same point, they connect after IN− too.
4. Join **VBUS (pin 6) to IN−** with a short jumper.
5. Connect the small pins: **pin 1 VCC → 3.3 V**, **pin 2 GND → ground**, **pin 3 SCL / pin 4 SDA → POWER J2 → MAIN J2**.
6. Leave the battery − / ground wiring exactly as it is.

```
Battery + -> switch -> IN+ [INA260] IN- -> buck input +   (VBUS joined to IN-)
Battery − -> ground (unchanged)
```

**Use thick wire (18 AWG or more) for steps 2 and 3.** If your module has screw terminals for IN+/IN−, use those.

**What gets measured:** only the loads wired **after** IN−. Anything wired directly to the battery or switch, like the ESC if it's still on the battery, won't be counted. That's fine; it just means less current goes through the INA260.

## Option 2 (no rewiring): use the INA260 only as a voltmeter
If you don't want to touch the thick wires:
1. Join **IN+, IN− and VBUS together** and connect them to the **switch output (+)** with a thin jumper. **Don't leave IN+ / IN− floating**; floating inputs have killed INA260 VBUS pins.
2. Wire VCC, GND, SCL and SDA as in Option 1.

It will show **battery voltage (and battery % from voltage)**, but **current stays 0**, so no power or charge/discharge reading. MAIN also already measures battery voltage through J3 (the 100k/22k divider), so you have a backup either way.

## Check before connecting the battery
- **IN+ to IN−** (Option 1): beep or almost 0 Ω. That's normal.
- **IN+, IN− and VBUS to GND:** must **not** beep.
- **Switch OFF:** the buck input must show **no** voltage.

You set this module to 9 V with a solder bridge on its back. But check your steering servos first.

## Setting 9 V
On the back are three small solder-jumper pads labelled **3.3V, 9V and 12V**. With none bridged, the output is the default **5 V** ("DEF 5V 5A").

1. Melt a blob of solder across the **9V pad only**, so its two halves join.
2. Make sure the **3.3V and 12V pads stay open**. Bridging two pads at once gives a wrong voltage.
3. Wire it up:
   - **IN +** goes to battery + (after the switch and INA260). **IN −** goes to GND.
   - **OUT +** is your 9 V. **OUT −** is GND; IN − and OUT − are the same ground.
   - The arrow on the back points from IN to OUT.
4. **Before connecting anything to the output**, power it and measure OUT + to OUT −. It should read about **9.0 V**. Only then connect the load.

## Two things to know before you use 9 V
**1. Your steering servos are 8 V parts.** The POWER board's M3 rail is meant to be **8 V** for the two A20CLM servos. This module can't make 8 V; it only offers 3.3, 5, 9 or 12 V. Check the maximum voltage printed on the servo or its datasheet:
- **If it says 8.4 V max:** don't feed it 9 V, or it may overheat or fail mid-flight. Use an **adjustable buck** set to 8.0 V for M3 instead.
- **If it says 9 V or higher is OK:** 9 V is fine.

**2. Your 3S battery is only "12 V" when full.** It reads 12.6 V when charged, about 11.1 V in the middle, and around 9–10 V near empty. A buck can't output more than its input, and it needs roughly 1 V of headroom. So the 9 V output **starts to sag once the battery falls below about 10 V**, which is about 3.3 V per cell. That's acceptable if you land before the battery is that low, but don't expect a steady 9 V down to empty.

## Using this module on the POWER board
The landing holes **M1–M5** have 3 holes: **1 = IN, 2 = GND, 3 = OUT**. Connect:
- module **IN +** → hole 1
- module **IN −** and **OUT −** → hole 2
- module **OUT +** → hole 3

Use the module's default 5 V (no pads bridged) for **M1** (5 V logic) and **M2** (5 V servo). This module has a 3.3 V pad, so it can also do **M4/M5**.
