**On field 2 ("time since initial power"):** that field is satisfied by the Teensy's own `millis()` counter — no RTC needed, and it literally cannot fail as long as the Teensy is running. **On "logging must survive even if Teensy hangs":** a flash chip has no brain of its own — something has to drive its SPI bus to write to it. If Teensy hangs, nothing writes to *any* flash, no matter which physical board it sits on. The thing that actually gives you "logging happens no matter what" is already in your architecture: **Board 3 (backup) has its own independent microSD and takes over telemetry** when the watchdog confirms Primary is dead. That's the real answer to your requirement — not where the flash chip physically sits.

Given that, **moving the flash chip off Board 1 onto a connector actually works against what you want**: it adds a cable, a connector, and (for a shared bus) a buffer chip — three new things that can fail, between the Teensy and the one piece of hardware you said must never lose data. So:

| Ref | What it actually does | Why no breakout can provide this |
|---|---|---|
| **C1** (10µF, +5V at J1) | Bulk reservoir at the board's power entry | The whole board is fed over a cable from Board 4; cable inductance means local bulk, not module-local decoupling, is what prevents sag |

| **C6** (10µF, U2) | Absorbs the LoRa TX current pulse | A breakout's own tiny decoupling cap wasn't sized for your TX burst current — you don't know its value, so don't assume it covers this |
| **C11** (100µF, U6) | Absorbs the Class-D amp's switching/speaker transient | Same logic — a beacon driving a real speaker needs real bulk capacitance nearby, a breakout's onboard cap is sized for logic, not audio current spikes |
| **C12** (100nF, U8 flash) | Flash write/erase current spikes | You're likely using a bare SOIC breakout here, not a "complete module" — cheap flash carriers often have **no** decoupling at all |
| **C13** (100nF, U10) | TPL5010 supply decoupling | **U10 is a bare chip you're soldering yourself.** There is no breakout. This is mandatory per TI's datasheet, full stop. |

Every resistor on this board exists for a reason a module can't solve on its own, because it's about *system* behavior, not any one chip:

| Ref | Why it's not optional |
|---|---|
| R1, R2, R3 (CS pull-ups) | Three devices share one SPI bus. Without these, at power-up — before firmware configures anything — all three chip-selects float, and more than one device can think it's selected at once. This has nothing to do with what's on any breakout. |
