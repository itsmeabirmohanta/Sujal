On the BMP585, **SDO picks the I²C address**. It isn't a data line here.

- SDO → GND gives address **0x46**
- SDO → 3V3 gives address **0x47**

The chip has to see one or the other, so SDO can't be left floating. If it floats, the address can change from one power-up to the next and the Teensy may not find the sensor.

**Why 3V3 and not GND:** your flight firmware ([Config.h:245](C:/Users/kalbh/Downloads/vritra-gcs-master/vritra-gcs-master/firmware/vritra_fc/Config.h:245)) has a comment saying 0x47 showed up in your own I²C scan and 0x46 didn't. That means SDO was already tied high on the board you tested. I copied that so Board 1 behaves the same as the board your firmware already works with.

**On Board 2 it's mainly for consistency.** The other chips on that bus are the LSM6DSO32 (0x6A) and the HDC3020 (0x44), so neither choice would clash. GND would work too, because the firmware tries 0x47 first, then 0x46, and checks the chip ID either way. I kept 3V3 so every BMP585 uses the same wiring and the same address.

Some breakout boards already tie SDO high or low with a resistor or solder jumper. If yours has a jumper set to GND, my 3V3 wire would short it. Check your module before you solder that wire.
