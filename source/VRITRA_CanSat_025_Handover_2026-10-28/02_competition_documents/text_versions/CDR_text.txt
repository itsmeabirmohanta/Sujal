1. CAN-7USAT MISSION OVERVIEW
We are team VRITRA with a team ID as 2026-INSPACe-CAN-7USAT-025 of Lovely Professional University.
We are developing a CAN-7USAT for the IN-SPACe CAN-7USAT India Student Competition 2026 which
includes designing, fabrication and flight of CANSAT. The CAN sized satellite will be carried to an altitude of
1000 m or ±100 m above the ground level (AGL) through a launch vehicle. It will be ejected from the rocket at
the apogee and should have a two-stage recovery system while transmitting real time telemetry to a ground
station.
Primary Mission Objective: This is the primary descent control system which is a hemispherical parachute
connected to the outer body with no ejection mechanism and is deployed by the atmospheric drag. It will be
activated directly after the ejection of CANSAT from the rocket having the descent rate of 20m/s with deviation
of ±5m/s.
Secondary Mission Objective: This is the secondary descent control system with a secondary deployment of a
RAM-air parachute at an altitude of 600 m AGL. This reduces the descent rate to 1-3 m/s at touchdown. It is
also an optional objective which covers precision, controlled flight and targeted landing and is demonstrated by
GPS guided RAM air parafoil which enables active recovery at the target landing point near launch pad.
This document presents the Comprehensive Design Review (CDR) of a CAN sized satellite designed by team
VRITRA.

4. SYSTEM OVERVIEW
4.1 Brief summary of the mission
CAN-7USAT is the entry for team VRITRA in IN-SPACe CAN-7USAT India student Competition 2026
which focuses on building a CAN sized satellite with two stage guided descent and full real time telemetry.
PARAMETER TARGET
Deployment altitude 1000 m (±100 m) AGL
Ejection shock survival ≥ 30G
Primary descent rate 20 m/s (±5 m/s)
Secondary activation altitude 600 m (±10 m) AGL
Secondary descent rate 1–3 m/s
Telemetry rate ≥1 Hz over LoRa
Stabilization Mechanical gyroscopic wheel
Table 5: Mission requirement
4.1.1 Mission flow
1. Ejection and Stabilization: In this stage the CANSAT separates from the rocket at apogee by surviving the
ejection shock and stabilizes itself using a mechanical gyroscopic wheel.
2. Primary Descent: After ejection from the rocket, the hemispherical shaped parachute is opened due to the
atmospheric drag without any ejection mechanism making the descent rate to 20 m/s ±5 m/s.
3. Guided secondary descent: At 600 meters above the ground level, a GPS guided RAM air parafoil is
deployed with the help of a servo-based ejection mechanism from the parachute bay. This design of parachute
is made in such way that it cuts down the descent rate from 20 m/s to 1-3 m/s and is actively steering towards
the launch site through the dual axis break line servo control.
4. Continuous telemetry: All 15 mandatory parameters are transferred with the help od LR1121 a LoRa at the
rate of 1 Hz. It also has a backup microSD card for storing of data in case of packet or link loss.
12
Team ID: 2026-INSPACe-CAN-7USAT-025
Throughout the flight, the CAN-7USAT continuously acquires data from its onboard sensor stack and transmits
all 15 mandatory telemetry parameters over a LoRa link at a minimum rate of 1 Hz, while simultaneously logging
the complete dataset to an onboard microSD card as a backup in case of telemetry loss. The avionics architecture
is built around a Teensy 4.1 primary flight computer running an 8-state finite state machine, supported by a
secondary backup avionics board with an independent processing board to ensure telemetry continuity in the
event of a primary processor failure.
Beyond the core mission, Team VRITRA also focusses on additional objectives of the mission which can help
us in scoring additional points. The bonus capabilities for extra jury weightage are:
• Onboard video capture (descent + ejection)
• NavIC-enabled multi-GNSS positioning
• Environmental sensor suite — PM2.5/PM10, lightning, UV, ozone, CO₂
• Innovation payload: biochemical characterization of atmospheric bacterial isolates collected
• Custom Python ground station for live telemetry, command uplink, and mission logging
4.2 Any changes made from PDR stage
1. Onboard CANSAT telemetry antenna has been changed from Rubber duck omnidirectional antenna
(AEACAC049018-S868) to Radio master T antenna of 865 MHz. This change was done due to dual band
requirement of frequency during telemetry. Team VRITRA has its primary frequency as 867.0 MHz and the
backup frequency as 86.75 MHz, which is important for telemetry transmission without any interference. T
antenna from Radio Master is a dual antenna which makes it easy for us to switch the frequency whenever
required.
2. Material of CANSAT has been changes which were Carbon fiber rods and glass fiber body. The body
material which is selected is Acrylonitrile Butadiene Styrene (ABS) filament which is a tough, impact-
resistant thermoplastic polymer. The plates are of aluminium and the rods are of glass fiber. This are changes
made in material procurement which was done after the design testing on Ansys. According to the results
the selected material gave better results as compared to the pervious in perspective of stress, etc.
3. The gyro wheel motor was changed from N20 to 2205 2300KV brushless DC motor. This was done after
testing our RAM air parachute. To avoid spinning of CANSAT after parachute deployment we needed
higher RPM motor than the N20.
The required test results are given below to justify about the changes made in CDR stage.

Reaction-Wheel Geometry and Moment of Inertia:
1.1 A Thin Ring (PRIMARY DESIGN)
The primary design assumes the reaction wheel is constructed as a thin ring (rim-weighted geometry).
Formula: I = mr²
Where: m = wheel mass = 0.070 kg
r = wheel radius = 0.035 m
Calculation:
r² = (0.035) ² = 0.001225 m²
I_wheel, ring = m × r²
I_wheel, ring = 0.070 × 0.001225
I_wheel, ring = 0.00008575 kg·m²
Final Result: I_wheel, ring = 8.575000e-5 kg·m² = 85.75 × 10⁻⁶ kg·m²
This is the primary moment of inertia used throughout the calculation report.

4.6 Material Procurement details
All components of team VRITRA are sources from combination of different domestic electronics suppliers and
international distributers. The main preference is given to the Indian manufactured parts where precision is better

4.6.1 The procurement strategy
• Primary domestic suppliers: Robu.in, Amazon India and local Indian electronics vendors for sensors,
connectors, and mechanical hardware.
• International/specialty suppliers: Digikey for components not readily available domestically (e.g., specific
IMU breakout variants, precision servos).
• Direct manufacturer sourcing: A significant share of the sensor and power-regulation stack such as
BMP585, ICM-20948, BMV080, AP64500, AP62301, and others, is sourced directly from 7Semi
Technologies, an Indian company based in Navi Mumbai specializing in embedded sensors and wireless
communication modules — these components are highlighted in the BOM as Make-in-India contributions.
• Structural materials: Glass fiber rods, plates, and sheet stock procured separately for the shell, base plate,
and internal structural elements.
• Procurement timeline: Component purchasing is scheduled as a dedicated milestone immediately after PDR
submission, ahead of PCB fabrication and mechanical fabrication, to avoid schedule slip on integration and
testing.

4.9 Pre-Flight Analysis
Pre-Fight Analysis talks about the expected performance envelope of CANSAT prior to launch by using
analytical models and simulation to predict system behaviour under different conditions.
4.9.1 Descent Trajectory & Wind Drift Analysis
A MATLAB-based simulation was run against expected launch-site wind conditions to understand about the
parafoil's guided descent trajectory. The simulation output shows a top-down ground track and altitude-vs-time
profile which is used to:
• Predict the parafoil's glide path and expected landing dispersion radius under the PID waypoint-steering
logic.
65
Team ID: 2026-INSPACe-CAN-7USAT-025
• Verify that the glide ratio (2.50) and forward speed (6.37 m/s) provide sufficient cross-range authority to
pay for given wind drift between 600 m activation and descent.
• Get an expected recovery search radius around the target waypoint for the ground recovery test.
Fig 37: MATLAB parachute testing
4.9.2 Communication Link Prediction
• Calculated link margin of 71.6 dB at 1 km (867 MHz, SF10) and is well above the 10 dB minimum needed
for reliable LoRa telemetry.
• No packet loss expected under nominal line-of-sight conditions between CanSat and ground station.
• Sufficient headroom to tolerate antenna misalignment, multipath, and orientation changes near touchdown.
• NETID/PANID isolation (0x98) and SF10 orthogonality expected to prevent cross-interference with other
teams' radios at the shared launch site.
4.9.3 Sensor Calibration Strategy
• Ground calibration (CMD_CALIBRATE) zeroes barometric altitude, gyroscope offset, and accelerometer
bias at pad level, immediately before flight.
• Eliminates day-to-day atmospheric pressure drift from affecting the 600 m secondary-deployment trigger.
• Removes IMU bias accumulated during transport and handling, ensuring gyro-wheel stabilization and
apogee detection operate against a true zero reference.
• Calibration is deliberately timed as late as possible in the pre-launch sequence to minimize the window in
which drift could re-accumulate before flight.

1. Sensing and error computation
The loop begins at START and, every cycle, executes:
• Read Sensors data of IMU orientation and GPS and gets current heading (IMU) and position (GPS).
• Compute Heading Error compares current heading/track against the target bearing to the landing point,

2. Off-course decision gate
• If No: It is kept Straight and no correction is applied and the loop proceeds directly to the stabilization
stage.
• If Yes: It falls through to the correction path and Run PID Controller by Calculating servo, which calculates
the required servo command from the heading error.
3. Differential brake actuation
Tells about turning to which branches on the sign of the PID output:
• If Left: Then pull the left line (left brake servo pulls down, right stays at/near trim) which is a asymmetric
drag induces a left turn.
• If Right: Then pull the right line (mirror case).
4. Stabilization and termination check
• The stabilize with Gyro Wheel happen after any steering command or after "Keep Straight" command.
Spinning the mechanical gyro to constant RPM before secondary parachute deployment so no reaction

• If Yes: Then the loop returns back to Read Sensors, repeating the whole cycle at 100 ms (10 Hz),

• If No: It is dismissed and landed.

The altitude descent control loop known as ADCL have multiple steps within it. The steps are:

1. Sensor acquisition and altitude estimation:
The loop starts at Start and initializes the two altitude sensors which are the BMP585 barometer and the L89
GNSS module. Each 100 ms cycle then performs the process as:
• Read Barometric Pressure (BMP585) and calculate barometric altitude, converting raw pressure to altitude
through the standard barometric formula.
• Read GPS Altitude from L89 GNSS independently of the barometric path.
• Sensor data fusion of BMP585 and GPS Altitude. This further combines the two independent altitude
values, giving idleness against single sensor failure (e.g., GPS dropout or barometer drift) and improving
accuracy over either source alone.
• Apply Kalman Filter & Noise [rejection] is used to smoothen the fused altitude estimate by rejecting sensor
noise and reducing estimation lag which matters for the timing-critical events downstream (600 m release,
ground impact).
• Transmit Altitude Telemetry (LoRa, 1 Hz) downlinks the filtered altitude at 1 Hz regardless of flight state,
so ground station tracking is continuous even though the decision logic runs faster (100 ms).
• Loop (100 ms cycle) closes the sensor-acquisition loop back to the pressure read by giving a 10 Hz internal
rate against a 1 Hz telemetry rate.
2. Flight-state decision chain
The filtered altitude provides three sequential decision diamonds in which each gating a distinct mission phase:
Gate 1: Is altitude increasing?
• If Yes: It is still ascending under boost/coast. State is set to ASCENT: Monitor Peak which then automatically
goes to DESCENT: Primary Parachute once apogee is detected in which the ascent state itself watches for
the peak and hands off to the descent state this is deployment trigger event from our recovery subsystem.
• If No: The altitude is not increasing (past apogee, under descent), so control falls through to Gate 2.
Gate 2: Altitude ≤ 600 m AGL?
• If Yes: State is set to AEROBRAKE_RELEASE, which triggers Activate PID Steering (GPS-Guided) that
is the software-side trigger for releasing primary parachute and enabling the guided parafoil descent under
closed-loop GPS/PID control.
• If No: It is still above 600 m, loop back through the "No" path to re-check on the next altitude update.
Gate 3: Altitude ≤ ground level?
• If Yes: The landing is detected. State is set to IMPACT: Activate Audio (locator beacon), and the state
machine terminates at End.

8.2 Housekeeping Subsystem
The housekeeping subsystem have the six supporting systems that keep CAN-7USAT structurally sound,
powered, communicating, sensing, actuating, and recovering. Full design and specifications for each are
presented in their respective sections given below.
8.2.1 Mechanical Subsystem
• Manufacturing approach — Shell and parachute bay are additively manufactured (3D printed) for
complex lattice geometry, while plates and rods are machined/cut for dimensional precision at load-bearing
interfaces.
• Assembly & maintainability — the rod-and-plate stack allows tool-less or minimal-tool disassembly,
enabling individual PCB layers to be pulled for testing, rework, or replacement without disturbing the rest
of the stack.
• Mass efficiency — the lattice/perforated shell pattern removes non-structural material to minimize weight
while the load-bearing members (rods, plates) retain full cross-section, keeping the structure light without
under-designing the critical path.
• Thermal/venting consideration — the perforated shell and lattice pattern also aid passive airflow around
internal electronics and allow clean strip-away of the parachute deployment bag without snag points.
87
Team ID: 2026-INSPACe-CAN-7USAT-025
• Alignment & repeatability — the four-rod symmetric layout constrains all plates to a fixed radial position,
ensuring consistent PCB stack alignment across builds and simplifying connector/wiring routing between
layers.
• Interface with recovery system — the parachute bay geometry and internal lid/spring mechanism is
mechanically hosted at the top of this stack, so the structure directly interfaces with the release device (R1)
and P2 deployment bag housing.

14. LOGISTICS AND TRANSPORTATION
14.1 CAN-7USAT transportation and handling
CAN-7USAT is designed to be transported and handled with minimum risk to its structural, electronic, and
descent-system integrity between Lovely Professional University and the competition launch site in
khushinagar, UP. The following handling protocol is followed by Team VRITRA:
14.1.1 Pre-Transport Preparation
• Battery will be disconnected/powered off via the external switch prior to transport, as per the competition's
power-safety requirement.
• Primary parachute and RAM-air parafoil will be packed into their respective bays and will be opened only
at the launch site immediately before integration to avoid canopy compression damage or line tangling over
an extended packed duration.
• The CANSAT will be placed in a custom-fitted protective transport case with foam cutouts matching its
dimensions, cushioning the structure against shock and vibration during vehicle transport.
• All exposed sensor apertures (GPS antenna, air-quality sensor inlets, UV sensor window) will be covered
with protective caps/tape during transit and removed only during final pre-launch checkout.
• Spare batteries, tools, and the Petri dish culture media for the biochemical payload will be transported
separately in a dedicated accessory case to avoid contamination or accidental damage to the primary
structure.
14.1.2 Transport Method
CAN-7USAT and the ground station equipment are transported by road from LPU to the launch site by team
members and mentors, using the protective case described above secured against movement in the vehicle.

16. CONCLUSION
Team VRITRA's CAN-7USAT presents a Critical Design Review that carries forward and matures every design
commitment made at PDR into a fabricated, analytically verified, and largely bench-tested flight vehicle. The
two-stage descent architecture of a drag-deployed primary parachute followed by a GPS-guided RAM-air
parafoil with dual-axis brake-line steering meets all mandatory descent-rate and activation-altitude requirements
with demonstrated margin, while the Teensy 4.1 avionics architecture, dual LR1121 telemetry links, and dual-
confirmed altitude triggers reflect a design cantered rather than single points of failure.
The communication link comes as 71.6 dB margin at the full 1 km design range even under the competition-
specified 867 MHz/SF10 configuration. Beyond mandatory compliance, CAN-7USAT differentiates itself
through its innovation payload of biochemical characterization of atmospheric bacterial isolates collected during
descent and also a five-sensor bonus environmental suite and NavIC-enabled dual-band GNSS, reflecting the
team's intent to contribute genuine atmospheric science value beyond the competition's baseline mission profile.
A small set of verification items such as full-scale vibration testing, field RF range confirmation, and power
budget revalidation remain open ahead of the Flight Readiness Review. Team VRITRA views these not as
design uncertainties but as the final confirmatory steps of a design that has already been carried through
structural analysis, FEA simulation, link budget calculation, and subsystem-level bench testing.
With eight team members contributing across aerospace, electronics, and robotics disciplines under faculty and
mentor guidance from Lovely Professional University, Team VRITRA submits this Critical Design Review
confident that CAN-7USAT is on track to meet every mission requirement of the IN-SPACe CAN-7USAT India
Student Competition 2026, and looks forward to demonstrating its performance at launch.
